PROJECT SUMMARY
A full brand refresh for Starise — an enterprise communication platform.
I had two months to take the brand from a vague brief to a shipped identity. I pitched progress to leadership every week until launch
PROBLEM STATEMENT
“Make it Americanized”
The old mark felt rooted in traditional enterprise. There wasn't much philosophy behind it, so the first challenge was figuring out what that brief could actually mean.
A new identity built around a stronger idea: a rice-like star (米) paired with a directional arrow. Fresh, modern, collaborative.
THE PROCESS
01
Sketch exploration
{{ p1Mark }}
One line of direction. No reference. No constraints.
I had to define what “Americanized” could mean for an enterprise communication platform before I could design for it.
I started on paper, exploring a dozen marks before anything went digital.

Designing for the person making the decision.
Instead of presenting one direction and asking for feedback, I put five variations into the first proposal so they could be compared side by side.
Two were my explorations; three came from another designer working on the project.

PROJECT SUMMARY
Taipei People reimagines Taiwanese literature through Mahjong gameplay and projection mapping.
Our team adapted the opening chapter, Eternal Snow Beauty, turning each Mahjong move into part of a projected narrative — bringing audiences into the fading world of Shanghai’s upper class.
PROBLEM STATEMENT
How do you make a classic literary work feel accessible without losing its character?
We wanted to retell a literary work for contemporary audiences who might never pick up the original book.
The answer was to let them play through the story rather than simply watch it.
THE PROCESS
01
Design Concept
{{ t1Mark }}
Mahjong became the narrative controller.
Each tile action — taking or throwing — triggers projected scenes, animation, and narration, gradually unfolding the story.
The experience culminates in a symbolic Four Happiness win: a metaphor for fleeting beauty and lost time.
02
Key Features
{{ t2Mark }}
A physical game, a projected story.
{{ f.t }}
{{ f.b }}
03
Storyboard
{{ t3Mark }}
The blueprint for the experience.
We mapped the narrative beats, Mahjong interactions, projected scenes, and transitions before translating them into animation.
04
UI & Animation
{{ t4Mark }}
Making the story readable while keeping it atmospheric.
I designed the projection UI and animation system around the physical Mahjong interaction, balancing narrative clarity with the mood of the original literature.
Hover to play · click to pause.
RESULT
Exhibition Day
A story you could play.
The final installation paired a tactile Mahjong set with projection mapping, turning a literary narrative into something audiences could physically interact with.
The novelty of manipulating real tiles while watching the story unfold became one of the experience’s strongest points.

PROJECT SUMMARY
I was asked to ship an AI feature for a banking-sector PoC. but, the product had no design system, no documentation, and years of inconsistent patterns baked into it.
PROBLEM STATEMENT
I could have designed the new feature on top of the existing UI. Instead, I first had to understand what was already there — and decide what was worth fixing before adding something new.
GOAL
Build and demo an AI Knowledge + IVR PoC across Concio and iManager, while creating a foundation the team could actually keep using.
THE PROCESS
01
Ambiguity
{{ a1Mark }}
Lead designer on leave — D-1 handover No existing component library No documentation Sketch → Figma migration task Design student, figuring it out alone
A D-1 handover and a blank Figma file.
I wasn’t starting with a clean brief. I was inheriting years of decisions.
02
Audit
{{ a2Mark }}
Before designing anything new, I needed to understand what was already broken.
I audited the existing flows and benchmarked familiar products including WhatsApp, LINE, Teams, and Slack.
Three findings came out of the audit.
Navigation had no labels · iconography had no system · padding varied across screens.
These weren’t just aesthetic issues They were the reason the platform felt hard to use
These weren’t just visual inconsistencies. They made the product harder to understand — and harder to extend.
03
Foundation
{{ a3Mark }}
Before the AI, I built the system.
I could have designed the new feature on top of the existing patterns. Instead, I decided to establish a reusable foundation first, so the PoC wouldn’t become another one-off.
Iconography
Unified styles, naming, states, and badges.
Components
Buttons, inputs, dropdowns, navigation, progress indicators.
Variables & instances
Structured for handoff and future scale.
Typography & colour
A consistent visual language across app and desktop.
BEFORE / AFTER
04
Prototype
{{ a4Mark }}
Concio — AI Knowledge
The existing app couldn’t be completely redesigned within the PoC timeline, so I worked within its constraints and focused the redesign where it mattered most.
Full AI feature walkthrough available upon request.
iManager — AI Knowledge + IVR
The same foundation was extended into the desktop experience, adapting the patterns for a more complex admin workflow.
Full walkthrough available upon request.
05
Tradeoffs
{{ a5Mark }}
Not everything made it into the PoC.
The timeline and management constraints meant some of the problems I identified stayed as findings rather than becoming product changes.
RESULT
Shipped, Adopted, Extended
AI PoC
Successfully demoed.
Design System
Adopted as the team’s Figma baseline.
Cross-platform
Foundation applied across Concio and iManager.
The AI Knowledge + IVR PoC was successfully demoed to a major banking-sector client.
A foundation that outlived the PoC
The component library and foundation I built from scratch became the team’s Figma design-system baseline — and is still in use today.
