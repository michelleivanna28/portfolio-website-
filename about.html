Michelle is in what she calls a "mild" identity crisis.
The symptoms are common enough: a suspicion that everyone else is further along, an internet built to confirm it, and a timeline she never actually agreed to.
She is slowly coming round to the idea that the timeline was invented, and that arriving late is mostly a matter of whose clock you were reading.
She is not past this. She simply no longer believes the premise.
Michelle is suspicious of shortcuts, and equally suspicious of years of experience as a measure of anything.
What she trusts instead: how quickly someone grasps a new thing, whether they can explain it back, whether they will wander into business or engineering to understand a problem properly.
Most of what she knows, she learned sideways.
Given the choice, she takes the harder route. It teaches more, and she is not in a hurry.
At three, Michelle's mother packed her bag and walked her into a Yamaha music school, as was the norm.
She stayed long enough to earn a diploma and has never known quite what to do with it. She was never the competition-winning kind. She kept going anyway.
When she moved to Taipei her Chinese had not caught up yet, so she joined the piano club because she needed somewhere to practice. She was the only foreigner. They listened to what each other was working on, and that turned out to be enough.
Somewhere in there the nagging stopped being necessary. Piano became a language of its own, and she could disappear into it.
Michelle needs to know the shape of a thing before she is comfortable inside it.
She plans. She tidies. She gets twitchy around a messy workflow or a baseless claim.
For a long time she thought this was a personality flaw with a useful professional side effect. It isn't. Noticing what is out of place is what caring looks like up close.
A former colleague put it as careful planning and complete ownership from start to finish. She owns every word.
Michelle's interests are scattered but not random. Philosophy, books, films, whatever the current hobby is. They tend to circle the same thing: how a person grows, and what it takes.
She will watch or read almost anything from Alain de Botton and The School of Life.
